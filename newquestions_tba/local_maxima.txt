Approach - naive counting, seems v tough, until we realize that since all permutations are considered, and all are equally likely, we can greatly simplify the problem.

see, any position i, when will it be a maxima? when its greater than its surrounding guys.

if i is 1 or n (the edges) - its essentially comparing x_i and x_i-1 (or x_i+1).

Since all permutations are equally likely, this is equivalent to saying i pick 2 random numbers a,b from 1-n.. whats the prob that a<b.

Clearly, its 1/2! (by symmetry.)

Now, for the non-edge cases, i can equivalently say, its the same as picking a,b,c. and whats the prob that b>a and b>c?

work it out.. all cases
a<b<c
a<c<b
b<a<c
b<c<a
c<a<b
c<b<a

(all combinations are equally likely).

clearly, b is the largest 2/6 times. hence the prob is 1/3.

so, for expected number of maximas, we have 2(1/2) + (n-2)(1/3) - num of each case into its prob.. all are independant- hence multiply and add for expectation.

below is a non-quick approach for fun, involving recursion.

alt approach, lets try a recursive method.. see if we can write E(n) in terms of E(n-1).

See, at every step, when we add a new "n" (which is clearly the biggest), it will add one new maxima definitely, but it may also remove older maximas. We need to quantify the removal of old maximas.
note that the new term has "n" places to enter, in the previous sequence of n-1 terms. 

How? if its at the ends - then it can only possibly remove the edge maximas.. 
and if its anywhere in the middle, it will affect 2 potential positions (to its either side).

Now, we can say that expected maximas in the prev seq were E(n-1)
and, since all positions equivalent, we can say that expected maximas per position are E(n-1)/(n-1).

now, when we add new term, with 2/n prob its at edge, and (n-2)/n prob its in a middle pos.

if at edge, it will remove exactly "1 expected maxima per position"
if at middle, it will remove "2 expected maxima per position".

this means, 

E(n) = 1 (ny default) + (2/n) (-E(n-1)/(n-1)) + (n-2)/n (-2E(n-1)/(n-1))  [explain each term.. im conditionally expecting and the 2* represnts 2 affected positions]

simplify this expected relation.
